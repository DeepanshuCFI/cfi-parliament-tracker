We asked every institution in Delhi's crash-compensation chain.
The non-answers are the audit.

Between October 2025 and February 2026, Crashfree India filed Right to Information applications across the institutions that owe crash victims a working compensation system — the Transport Department, the legal services authorities, Delhi Police districts, the Motor Accident Claims Tribunals in every district court complex, and the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways. This page publishes every application, every reply, and a grade for every response. Each claim links to the scanned reply it comes from.

01 · What the replies reveal

Six findings, in the system's
own words.

Every figure below is taken directly from an authority's written RTI reply, linked beside the claim so you can read the source document yourself.

2

The Supreme Court's fast-track machinery is functionally dead in Delhi.

The FDAR/CTAP/MCTAP scheme from Rajesh Tyagi v. Jaibir Singh produced two disposals in five years across every MACT court that answered — one at Patiala House, one at Dwarka. Every other court reported nil, or that no data exists.

Patiala House reply · Dwarka reply · West Tis Hazari reply

2%

2% of accident reports reached the tribunal on time.

Central district's MACT-01 disclosed that from 2020 to 2025, police filed 32 Detailed Accident Reports within the 30-day scheme deadline — and 1,543 beyond it. Insurers settled within 30 days of the DAR in 48 cases, and contested 392.

Legal aid for crash victims barely exists.

North-East Delhi's legal services authority recorded zero legal-aid applications from motor accident victims in three years. East recorded 10, North-West 28. DSLSA confirmed no MACT-specific lawyer panel exists and no SOP for motor-accident legal aid. Shahdara has one panel lawyer.

DLSA North-East · DSLSA · DLSA Shahdara

3,100+

Cases stuck over three years — in reporting courts alone.

East district: 586 of 1,840 pending MACT cases are over three years old. At Central MACT-02, 47% of all disposals took more than two years; 19% took more than five. Several districts did not report at all.

East Karkardooma · Central

5 of 7

Police districts stonewalled the accident-report question.

Five of seven Delhi Police district replies used a near-identical template: the information is “voluminous — come inspect the records yourself” under s.7(9). No district would state how many DARs it filed within the legal deadline.

0

Nobody at district level owns the hit-and-run scheme.

Four of five district legal services authorities replied that the Hit-and-Run Compensation Scheme, 2022 “does not pertain to this authority.” Only DLSA East reported beneficiaries: 29 death and 22 grievous-hurt payouts. Awareness campaigns for crash victims, 2021–24: zero in every district that answered.

DLSA West · DLSA East · DLSA North-West

The deeper finding: almost no institution maintains the registers the Supreme Court's own compliance framework (Rajesh Tyagi; Gohar Mohammed) presupposes — disposal-time statistics, DAR-timeliness logs, delay-reason records. "No such data maintained" is the single most common answer in this corpus. A system that does not measure itself cannot be held to its own deadlines.

05 · Method & sources

How to read
this audit.

What this is

A companion to Road Safety in Parliament. Parliament questions interrogate the executive from above; RTI applications interrogate it from below. Both are the same accountability instrument: a structured question, a named authority, a legal duty to answer. Where Parliament receives polished aggregates, RTI replies expose the actual state of record-keeping.

Filing and legal frame

Applications were filed under s.6(1) of the Right to Information Act, 2005 by Crashfree India (October–December 2025), with replies received through February 2026. Grades reference the Act: s.7(1) 30-day reply clock, s.7(9) form-of-access deflections, s.6(3) transfers, and the Delhi District Court (Right to Information) Rules, 2008 exemptions cited by two court complexes.

Figures and verification

Every figure quoted on this page is taken from the linked scanned reply, so it can be checked against the source in one click. Figures whose OCR extraction from poor-quality scans is not yet humanly verified are excluded from this page and flagged in the open dataset. Numbers are as stated by the responding authorities; Crashfree India has not adjusted them.

Privacy

Published documents are redacted copies: the applicant's personal contact details have been removed at pixel level. Officials' names and designations appear as they do on the public record.

Open data

The full structured dataset — thread lifecycle, response grades, extracted figures with confidence flags — is public under CC BY 4.0.
